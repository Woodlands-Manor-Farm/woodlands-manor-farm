Wonderful news for Bude and everyone who loves it: the town has once again been awarded **three Blue Flags for 2026**, which means Bude holds three of just ten Blue Flags handed out across the whole of Cornwall. Bude has done the triple.

So which beaches in Bude are Blue Flag in 2026? Three of them: **Summerleaze, Crooklets and Widemouth Bay**. Each has earned the prestigious Blue Flag Award, and it is a real boost for the town, because so much of our local economy relies on Bude's reputation as a destination for beautiful beaches and a glorious natural environment. As Bude Area Tourist Board put it, "We're a tourist town and to have our excellent beaches recognised benefits everyone."

If you are planning a stay at Woodlands Manor Farm, all three are within easy reach, so here is a closer look at each one, plus what the Blue Flag actually means.

## What makes a Blue Flag beach?

The [Blue Flag](https://www.keepbritaintidy.org/professional-services/blue-flag-award-beaches-and-marinas) is one of the world's most recognised marks of a clean, safe and well-managed beach, managed in England by Keep Britain Tidy. It is awarded against a demanding set of standards, and because it is re-assessed every single year, a beach can lose it if its water quality drops after a storm or its facilities fall into disrepair. Holding three is no small achievement. Here is what a beach has to get right.

**Water quality.** This is the big one. The beach is independently sampled through the season to test for bacterial contamination, such as E. coli and intestinal enterococci, and it must reach the very highest "excellent" standard. It must also be free from sewage-related and industrial discharges.

**Environmental management.** The beach has to be kept clean and clear of litter and marine debris, with plenty of well-maintained bins and recycling, and clean public toilets with controlled sewage disposal. Sensitive areas like sand dunes, coastal zones and wildlife habitats must be actively protected, and there are controls on dogs, which is why the main town beaches have seasonal bans in summer.

**Safety and services.** Qualified lifeguards and lifesaving equipment must be in place through the bathing season, with first aid easily to hand, clear and safe access, and conflicting activities like surfing and swimming zoned apart to prevent accidents. A proper emergency plan has to be ready for any pollution or safety incident.

**Environmental education and information.** A prominent board must display the local ecosystems, the latest water quality data, the beach facilities and the Blue Flag code of conduct. On top of that, the beach has to run a programme of environmental activities through the year, things like guided nature walks, beach cleans and workshops.

Earning three of these is down to a huge community effort. Local groups and the council work hard together to keep the beaches clean and the open spaces cared for, to provide bins and litter collection, and to help visitors and locals understand and protect the beach environment. It is everyone playing their part toward a genuinely sustainable place to visit.

There is one part of the award, though, that the town cannot control on its own: **water quality**. There is more to be done to improve it and to manage the impact of heavy rainfall, which can cause run-off and sewer overflow into the sea. It is an honest challenge, and one worth keeping an eye on (more on that below).

## Summerleaze: the family favourite with the famous sea pool

![Colourful beach huts and golden sand at Summerleaze Beach, Bude, on a sunny summer day](/images/blog/bude-blue-flag-beaches-2026/summerleaze-beach-huts.webp)

Right on the edge of town, **Summerleaze** is the beach most people picture when they think of Bude, and it was even [voted one of the UK's top 50 beaches](/summerleaze-beach-top-20-uk-beach-l/). At low tide it opens out into a huge expanse of golden sand, framed by dramatic layered rocks and a row of cheerful, candy-coloured beach huts.

Its crowning glory is the [**Bude Sea Pool**](https://www.budeseapool.org/), a semi-natural tidal pool tucked beneath the cliffs. Filled and refreshed by the sea, it offers a calmer place to swim and paddle away from the Atlantic surf, and it is a firm favourite with families and [wild swimmers](/wild-swimming-delights-near-woodlands-manor-farm/) alike. Lifeguards patrol in the main season, there is parking close by, and the shops and cafés of Bude are a short stroll away, which makes it a brilliant all-day base. It is the easiest of the three for a relaxed [family beach day](/top-10-reasons-why-bude-is-perfect-for-family-holidays-in-cornwall/).

## Crooklets: surf, sunsets and lifesaving heritage

![Surfers and lifeguards on the beach near Bude](/images/bude/surf-lesson-bude.jpg)

Just around the headland from Summerleaze, **Crooklets** is Bude's spirited surf beach and the home of the town's surf lifesaving tradition. Catch it at low tide and the sand stretches for miles, with rock pools to explore and some of the best waves in the area rolling in.

It is a wonderful spot to learn to surf, to watch the lifesavers train, or simply to settle in for one of Bude's famous west-facing sunsets. If you are new to the waves, our guide to the [surf schools in Bude](/local-surf-schools-bude/) will point you in the right direction.

## Widemouth Bay: the big, wide surf-and-sand beach

![The wide sands of Widemouth Bay near Bude at sunset](/images/blog/bude-blue-flag-beaches-2026/widemouth-sunset.webp)

A couple of miles south of the town, **Widemouth Bay** (locals say "Widmouth") is the broadest of the three: a long, open sweep of sand backed by low cliffs and rock pools. When the tide goes out it becomes an enormous playground, perfect for families spreading out, flying kites, rock-pooling and paddling.

It is also one of the best-loved surf beaches on this stretch of coast, popular with everyone from first-timers to seasoned surfers, and the wide horizon makes for spectacular sunsets. There are dog-friendly areas here too, so four-legged members of the family needn't miss out.

## Beyond the Blue Flags: wild beaches worth a visit

Not every beautiful beach near Bude carries a Blue Flag, and that is rather the point. Some of the most special stretches of this coast are wild, undeveloped National Trust coves that do not have the car parks, cafés and full-season lifeguard cover the award requires. They trade facilities for drama and quiet, and several are the closest beaches of all to Woodlands, among our favourite [local beaches](/woodlands-manor-farm-local-beaches/).

### Sandymouth

A few miles north, Sandymouth is a magnificent [National Trust beach](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/cornwall/sandymouth) that opens into a vast run of sand at low tide beneath towering, folded cliffs. There is a National Trust car park and café, seasonal RNLI lifeguard cover through the main summer weeks, and some of the best rock pools around. It is not a Blue Flag beach, but for sheer wild beauty and space it is hard to beat.

### Northcott Mouth

Closer to the farm, Northcott Mouth is a rugged cove where the pebbles give way to a broad sweep of sand at low tide. It gets seasonal RNLI lifeguard cover through the peak weeks (roughly mid-July to early September), though strong rip currents mean you should only swim when the red and yellow flags are flying. There is a National Trust car park and a much-loved seasonal tea caravan, but no toilets on site. Time your visit for low tide and you can make out the rusting metal ribs of the SS Belem shipwreck in the sand, and the rock pools are superb. Dogs are welcome all year.

### Duckpool

Our nearest beach, just two miles down the lane, Duckpool is a dramatic National Trust cove at the foot of the Coombe Valley. It is wild and unlifeguarded, with strong rip currents and rocks, so it is one for rock-pooling, paddling at the edge, beachcombing and soaking up the scenery rather than swimming. There is a National Trust car park and toilets nearby, and dogs are welcome year-round, which makes it a firm favourite for our guests on a morning walk straight from the farm.

**A word of caution on the wild beaches:** with no lifeguard cover, or only seasonal cover, take extra care with tides and currents. Swim only where and when it is safe, and keep an eye on the time as the tide comes in fast on this coast.

## A quick, honest word on water quality

The one thing none of us can fully control is what the weather and the sewer network do after heavy rain. Before a swim, it is always worth a quick check of the latest water quality. The [**Surfers Against Sewage Safer Seas & Rivers Service**](https://www.sas.org.uk/) app gives real-time alerts for Bude's beaches, so you can swim with confidence and plan around any overflow warnings. It takes a moment and means you always know before you go.

## Dogs on the beach: a note on the seasonal rules

Bude's town beaches, **Summerleaze and Crooklets**, carry a seasonal dog ban through the main summer season (roughly Easter to the end of September), though dogs are welcome out of season. **Widemouth Bay** has dog-friendly areas, and our nearest beach, wild and National Trust-owned **Duckpool**, welcomes dogs all year. ## Bude's beaches: your questions answered

**Which beaches in Bude have Blue Flags in 2026?**
Three: Summerleaze, Crooklets and Widemouth Bay. Together they make up three of only ten Blue Flags awarded across the whole of Cornwall.

**What makes a beach a Blue Flag beach?**
A beach has to meet strict standards for water quality (reaching the "excellent" grade), environmental management, safety and services (including lifeguards in season), and environmental education. It is re-assessed every year, so a beach can lose the award if standards slip.

**Is Duckpool or Sandymouth a Blue Flag beach?**
No. Duckpool, Sandymouth and Northcott Mouth are wild, National Trust-managed coves without the full facilities and lifeguard cover the award requires. They are not Blue Flag beaches, but they are beautiful, quieter spots for rock-pooling, dog walks and dramatic scenery. Take care with currents, as they have little or no lifeguard cover.

**Are dogs allowed on Bude's beaches?**
Summerleaze and Crooklets have a seasonal dog ban through the main summer season (roughly Easter to the end of September), and welcome dogs out of season. Widemouth Bay has dog-friendly areas, and Duckpool and Northcott Mouth welcome dogs all year.

**Which Bude beach is best for families?**
Summerleaze is the easiest for a family day out, with its tidal sea pool, seasonal lifeguards, beach huts and parking, all a short stroll from the town centre.
