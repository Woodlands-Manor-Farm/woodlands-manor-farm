Wonderful news for Bude and everyone who loves it: the town has once again been awarded **three Blue Flags for 2026**, which means Bude holds three of just ten Blue Flags handed out across the whole of Cornwall. Bude has done the triple.

Three of our much-loved beaches, **Widemouth Bay, Crooklets and Summerleaze**, have each earned the prestigious Blue Flag Award. It is a real boost for the town, because so much of our local economy relies on Bude's reputation as a destination for beautiful beaches and a glorious natural environment. As Bude Area Tourist Board put it, "We're a tourist town and to have our excellent beaches recognised benefits everyone."

If you are planning a stay at Woodlands Manor Farm, all three are within easy reach, so here is a closer look at each one, plus what the Blue Flag actually means.

## What is a Blue Flag, and why does it matter?

The Blue Flag is an international mark of a well-run, clean and well-cared-for beach. To fly one, a beach is assessed against a long list of criteria, including:

- **Safety and services**, such as first aid and lifeguard cover where needed.
- **Environmental information**, including clear details about the local ecosystems and how to look after them.
- **Water quality**, which must meet the "excellent" bathing water standard. (The related Seaside Award asks for the "sufficient" standard.)
- **Environmental management**, covering litter, waste and the general care of the beach and its surroundings.

Earning three of them is down to a huge community effort. Local groups and the council work hard together to keep the beaches clean and the open spaces cared for, to provide bins and litter collection, and to help visitors and locals understand and protect the beach environment. It is everyone playing their part toward a genuinely sustainable place to visit.

There is one part of the award, though, that the town cannot control on its own: **water quality**. There is more to be done to improve it and to manage the impact of heavy rainfall, which can cause run-off and sewer overflow into the sea. It is an honest challenge, and one worth keeping an eye on (more on that below).

## Summerleaze: the family favourite with the famous sea pool

![Summerleaze Beach, Bude, on a sunny summer day with colourful beach huts](/images/bude/bude-sea-pool.jpg)

Right on the edge of town, **Summerleaze** is the beach most people picture when they think of Bude. At low tide it opens out into a huge expanse of golden sand, framed by dramatic layered rocks and a row of cheerful, candy-coloured beach huts.

Its crowning glory is the **Bude Sea Pool**, a semi-natural tidal pool tucked beneath the cliffs. Filled and refreshed by the sea, it offers a calmer place to swim and paddle away from the Atlantic surf, and it is a firm favourite with families and wild swimmers alike. Lifeguards patrol in the main season, there is parking close by, and the shops and cafés of Bude are a short stroll away, which makes it a brilliant all-day base. It is the easiest of the three for a relaxed family beach day.

## Crooklets: surf, sunsets and lifesaving heritage

![Surfers and lifeguards on the beach near Bude](/images/bude/surf-lesson-bude.jpg)

Just around the headland from Summerleaze, **Crooklets** is Bude's spirited surf beach and the home of the town's surf lifesaving tradition. Catch it at low tide and the sand stretches for miles, with rock pools to explore and some of the best waves in the area rolling in.

It is a wonderful spot to learn to surf, to watch the lifesavers train, or simply to settle in for one of Bude's famous west-facing sunsets. If you are new to the waves, our guide to the [surf schools in Bude](/local-surf-schools-bude/) will point you in the right direction.

## Widemouth Bay: the big, wide surf-and-sand beach

A couple of miles south of the town, **Widemouth Bay** (locals say "Widmouth") is the broadest of the three: a long, open sweep of sand backed by low cliffs and rock pools. When the tide goes out it becomes an enormous playground, perfect for families spreading out, flying kites, rock-pooling and paddling.

It is also one of the best-loved surf beaches on this stretch of coast, popular with everyone from first-timers to seasoned surfers, and the wide horizon makes for spectacular sunsets. There are dog-friendly areas here too, so four-legged members of the family needn't miss out.

## A quick, honest word on water quality

The one thing none of us can fully control is what the weather and the sewer network do after heavy rain. Before a swim, it is always worth a quick check of the latest water quality. The **Surfers Against Sewage Safer Seas & Rivers Service** app gives real-time alerts for Bude's beaches, so you can swim with confidence and plan around any overflow warnings. It takes a moment and means you always know before you go.

## Dogs on the beach: a note on the seasonal rules

Bude's town beaches, **Summerleaze and Crooklets**, carry a seasonal dog ban through the main summer season (roughly Easter to the end of September), though dogs are welcome out of season. **Widemouth Bay** has dog-friendly areas, and our nearest beach, wild and National Trust-owned **Duckpool**, welcomes dogs all year.
